# Employee foundation

Current generic hiring, specific job assignment and schema 9 are described in [Employee jobs](EMPLOYEE_JOBS.md). [Employee hiring](EMPLOYEE_HIRING.md) retains the earlier names/hiring history.

Employees are owned by a company and plant. Co-op members see and manage the same roster; independent businesses get their own seeded staff. Entity keys contain company, plant and local employee ID, so matching local IDs never identify another business employee.

Machine operators are saved once in FactoryState.Operators. Existing Walker welder and Driver forklift records are projected into the same roster without creating extra persistent employees. A driver assigned to a forklift is seated; off-duty and newly hired reserve drivers stand at safe waiting locations. Boarding and leaving a parked vehicle are immediate attachment changes for now. Hiring and payroll are free for testing; skills and shifts remain later work.

The server routes operators at 110 cm/s along pedestrian lanes and short clear workstation spurs. Machines require an assigned operator physically at their station; unassigning or blocking an operator preserves the current reserved material/cycle and pauses its progress. Reassignment resumes it after the operator arrives. Idle operators return to their own waiting location. Existing workshop/migrated employees wait at their stations; newly provisioned starter and upgrade operators walk from a separate waiting spot. Operators stay with a paused part until the machine resumes. Welders reuse the existing real zone assembly progression. Poses reflect actual simulation work, walking, handling or waiting. The visual component can later accept supplied skeletal models without replacing employee identity or authority.

Assignments validate the target plant and connected player company permission on the server. Operator slots accept one employee per installed machine. Welders accept existing work zones; drivers accept an owned, parked, unoccupied forklift and cannot be removed or reassigned during an active pallet or return-to-parking trip. Zero means off duty; forklift vehicle IDs, route configuration and cargo/job identity survive employee swaps. Physical poses/roles and fictional names are public world state; financial data continues using company-private operations replication.

The earlier schema 7 added operator walking state and walker home/heading/speed/retry data. Schema 6 migration appends operators at the existing machines, preserves driver/welder IDs, authored floor paint and active transport/cycles, and is idempotent. Migration never inserts a new painted corridor across a player-authored work zone. Older schema 5 first uses the existing schema 6 migration. Saves during walking preserve path and progress. Normal user saves are not changed by acceptance tests.

Traffic uses marked crossing priority and physical clearance at unmarked machine spurs; employees maintain separation and can escape an existing overlap. Routes retry once per simulation second when unavailable. This is a small bounded factory simulation, not full crowd avoidance. Dynamic human-player yielding, animated forklift boarding/disembarking and pedestrian sidestepping in a narrow head-on meeting remain later work. Opposing employees may wait for a clear route instead of passing through each other. Placement clearance and authored workstation reach still require native visual review before claiming final model quality.
